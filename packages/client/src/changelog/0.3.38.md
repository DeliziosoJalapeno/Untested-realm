2026-09-07

#### Interface

* **Rematch!** When an online game ends, both players are now offered a rematch. Say yes and, as soon as your opponent also says yes (no need to wait out the timer), you're both taken **back to deck selection** for a fresh game — pick the same deck or a different one, lock it in, and the new match begins the moment both of you have chosen. The offer is part of the game state, so it survives a tab switch or a dropped connection: reconnect and it's still there. Either player declining — or letting the minute run out — simply closes the offer.
* **Route picker fixed!**  Now always showing routes to pick when needed
* **Walls are drawn on site borders again.** Wall of Fire / Air / Ice / Brambles are edge auras — they sit on the **border between two sites** (a 2×1 aura, which is also exactly what an Enchantress-animated wall occupies), not on a single site. A rendering regression had them showing as a flat 1×1 tint over one site; they're now back to a glowing bar straddling the correct border.
* **The Earthquake rearrange panel is much clearer.** The tiles are now large and **landscape** (sites are wide cards, so their art — default or promo — shows horizontally, as on the board), each site shows the units and artifacts standing on it (which travel with it when you swap), and the 2×2 is laid out in **board orientation** — sites appear exactly where they sit on your board, whether you're the first or second player.
* **Cards whose casting cost is changed show their real cost in your hand.** When a standing effect changes what a spell costs no matter how you cast it — e.g. **Court of Equity** ("Elite spells cost an additional ①, Uniques ②") — the card now shows its **actual current cost** as a ◆ badge in the top-left corner while it's in your hand (tinted red if it costs more than printed, green if less), on both desktop and mobile. Only unconditional changes trigger it: cost breaks that depend on where you cast (a specific site/square) aren't counted, since they only apply once you choose that target. Your opponent never sees your hand's cost badges.

#### General engine fixes

* **Being pulled or dragged now moves you square by square — it isn't a teleport.** Forced multi-step movement (Grapple Shot, Meat Hook, harpoons, a riptide's current — any push/pull/drag that spans more than one square) now actually *travels* the squares between where you start and where you end up, exactly like the rulebook says: only a true Teleport "moves directly without taking any steps in between." So a unit dragged across a **Briar Patch**, a flipped **Druid**'s thorns, a **Dark Alley**, or into a **Giant Shark**'s waters triggers each of those as it passes through, instead of skipping straight past them. The drag **stops** the moment the unit can't enter the next location — a gap in the board (the void) or a forced-entry ban like **Gnome Hollows** or **Bailey** — coming to rest on the last square it could reach. As before, forced movement **ignores the unit's own movement limits** (Immobile, Walls, and the like), and a unit is **not "at rest" while it's being dragged**, so a **Hillock Basilisk** / **Stone-Gaze Gorgons** gaze can't freeze it in a square it's only passing through — only once it stops there.
* **Turn triggers resolve one at a time.** When several of your start- or end-of-turn effects go off together, each one now resolves **completely — including any choice it asks you to make** — before the next one in the order begins, instead of all of them firing before the first prompt was answered.
* **Damage prevention follows the rulebook's two steps, and you choose the order.** Damage is now worked out in the proper sequence: first everything that **changes** the amount (increases, doublings, caps), then everything that **prevents** it (reductions, "takes less", immunities, and Ward). When one of your units has **two or more** different prevention effects that could apply to the same hit — say a **Makeshift Barricade** protecting it *and* its own Ward — you're asked which order to spend them in (the same "arrange these" prompt used for end-of-turn triggers), so you can break the Barricade and keep the Ward or vice-versa. Prevention stops the instant the damage reaches zero, so a spare shield isn't wasted; and you're only asked when the order actually matters (not for two identical Barricades, and not when every effect is just a Ward).
* **The "Battle of …" recap waits until the battle is really over.** When a fight pauses for a mid-combat choice (a prevention-order pick, a Bodyguard redirect), the two-sided battle summary no longer pops up early with a half-finished board — it now appears once the battle has fully resolved. It also always shows the **actual creature that was attacked** on the defending side, even if every point of damage was prevented.

#### Performance

* **The game engine is much faster — especially against the computer.** Working out a unit's current abilities (Airborne, Ward, power bonuses from nearby lords, and so on) was scanning the whole board every single time it was asked — and it's asked *constantly*, for movement, combat, and every step of the computer opponent's thinking. On a crowded board that one lookup could take several milliseconds and pile up into slow, stuttery turns; in a stress test of eight computer-vs-computer games, one game even ran long enough to hit our 30-second-per-match safety cutoff. That lookup is now effectively instant (it only consults the handful of cards that actually grant or remove abilities, not all of them), so turns resolve smoothly and **no game hit the cutoff after the fix** — with identical results, just faster. The route-path search got the same treatment and is roughly **20× quicker** on a busy board.



#### Card fixes

* **Carried artifacts can be buried too.** Burial effects — **Cave-In**, **Earthquake**, and the Editor's bury/submerge — now also affect artifacts a unit is carrying: if the bearer is buried it rides down with them; if the bearer can't be buried (an Avatar, a straddling oversized unit), the artifact is detached and buried on its own. (**Bury** already did this.)
* **Royal Bodyguard now actually offers to take the hit.** "If a nearby Avatar or royalty would take damage, Royal Bodyguard may take that damage instead" is now a real choice — you're **prompted** at the moment the damage would land (even for simultaneous combat damage) to redirect it onto the Bodyguard, instead of it doing nothing. If several royals would be hit at once (say a King *and* your Avatar), you're asked for each, and the Bodyguard soaks the **whole total in a single blow** — so it protects them all rather than dying after the first. (Replaces the old, easy-to-miss "guard duty" toggle.)
* **Hillock Basilisk / Stone-Gaze Gorgons don't freeze a minion mid-entry.** Their "at rest" disable no longer bites a minion while it is still entering the realm — its Genesis (including any choice it prompts you for) resolves in full first. So an **Archangel Michael** cast in front of a Basilisk takes his step and strikes it (keeping his Ward through his own action), instead of being disabled on arrival. A minion is only disabled once it actually comes to rest.
* **A Desert can damage minions atop its own site — including a voidwalker you cast it under.** A Desert's Genesis ("Deal 1 damage to each minion atop target nearby site") counts its **own** site as nearby, and a minion still lingering in the void of that now-sited square is atop it — so playing a Desert under a voidwalker damages it. 
* **"Reveal" effects actually reveal now.** A whole family of cards that say to *reveal* cards were doing it silently, so your opponent never saw what came up. Fixed across the board: **Assorted Animals** (each Beast it fetches), **Dhol Chants** (the spells it turns up), **Mother Nature** and **Lilith** (the topmost spell — Lilith the opponent's), **Pigs of the Sounder** and **Squeakers** (the five spells they dig up), **Pnakotic Manuscript** (the spell you draw), and **Searing Truth** (both spells drawn). A card that gets **summoned** off the top (Mother Nature/Lilith seducing a minion, the Boars and Pigs) is shown by the summon itself, so it isn't double-revealed — only the cards that *aren't* played are surfaced. (The spellbook tutors — Common Sense, Call to War, Highland Princess, Demonic Contract, Black Mass — already revealed correctly.)
* **Displace now works on minions, artifacts, AND auras** — its full printed text ("Teleport target minion, artifact, or aura one diagonal step"). It only ever moved minions before. Now you can also displace an artifact — a **carried** one is knocked off its bearer and **dropped** onto the destination site — or any aura, picked by clicking it on the board. The destination picker matches how each is placed: a minion/artifact/single-site aura moves to a diagonal **site**; a **2×2 aura** moves to a diagonal **intersection** (shown as intersection markers, Magellan-aware); and a **wall** slides to a nearby **site border** (shown as border hotspots).
