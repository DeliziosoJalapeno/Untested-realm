2026-11-14
#### Learn to play ####
* **New: a tutorial series** — open **🎓 Learn to play** on the main menu. A coach explains one rule at a time and then has you do it yourself on a prepared board, with the real rules engine. It points at the cards and squares involved, and tells you why if you try something else.
* **15 lessons in six chapters**:
  * *First steps* — the realm and sites; mana, threshold and minions; move and attack; life, Death's Door and the death blow.
  * *On the defensive* — defending and intercepting.
  * *Keywords and regions* — Airborne and Ranged; Burrowing, Submerge and the layers under a site; Lethal, Charge and Stealth.
  * *Spells* — magic and projectiles; artifacts and auras; Spellcasters.
  * *New rules* — **traps** (playing a face-down site and springing it), the **collection**, and Disabled / Outerbound / Protected.
  * *Graduation* — a full game, Frost Mage against the coach's Lavamancer, with advice for the position in front of you.
* Lessons can be taken in any order, restarted at any point, and the list remembers which ones you've finished (on this device).

#### Puzzles ####
* **New: 🧩 Puzzles** on the main menu — "win this turn" positions in the style of the old card-magazine puzzle pages. A board, a hand, one turn; the opposing Avatar is at Death's Door, and the opponent defends as well as the rules allow.
* **Eight puzzles**, easiest first, built around projectiles and blockers, the Toolbox and the collection, both kinds of trap, forcing a defender, and the Frost Mage's ice.
* Each puzzle has hints you can reveal one at a time and a written solution that explains why the tempting line fails.
* The defender is played by a solver that searches your whole turn: if any answer stops your attack, it finds it.

#### A Claude opponent ####
* **New difficulty: ✳ Claude.** The computer's moves are chosen by Claude, Anthropic's AI model. It is told the position from its own seat (it can't see your hand), given every legal move, and shown the search bot's choice as advice; it answers with its moves for the turn and a one-line plan, which is shown while it plays.
* The search bot remains the fallback: if Claude can't be reached, takes too long or picks nothing legal, the search bot's move is played and the note says so.
* It needs an Anthropic API key on the server (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, in the environment or a `.env` file — see `.env.example`). Without one the option is greyed out. By default only this machine and the admin account can use it.
* Cheaper and better informed: Claude is now asked only when there is a real decision (not for housekeeping questions, and not when all it could do is pass); the move list is trimmed to the moves that matter; every attack says which enemy units could defend it; and the rules plus its own deck's card text are sent in a form the API can cache. The plan note shows how many calls and tokens the game has used.
* **A much stronger Claude opponent.** Every move it is offered now comes with a simulation run on the game engine — "this move, then the opponent's best reply turn": life lost, units lost on each side, and how the position compares with passing. It also sees the search engine's whole line for the turn. If its chosen move still looks like a blunder next to the engine's, it is shown the simulated consequences and asked once more; a move the simulation says loses the game outright is replaced. It keeps a standing game plan across turns, and thinks harder by default.
* **Thinking bot fix:** when the search looked ahead past the end of its own turn, the modelled opponent stopped at its start-of-turn draw, so the opponent's reply was never actually played out. It is now.
* **Save a Claude game for review:** the plan note has a "⬇ Save game for review" button. It downloads a file with everything Claude was shown and chose that game, what the search engine would have played, any engine objections, and the game log.

#### Computer opponents learn from real games ####
* **Card statistics from Play Sorcery Online.** The computer opponents now use win-rate statistics from about 32,000 Constructed games, kindly published by [Play Sorcery Online](https://playsorceryonline.com/). A copy is stored with the app and refreshed only when a new release comes out (twice a month).
* **Mulligans:** the bot now also sends back cards that do measurably worse in an opening hand than in the deck (up to three, worst first).
* **Claude opponent:** cards with a clearly high or low record are marked in its deck reference.
* **Fix:** the bot no longer uses Sinkhole on its own site when no enemy site is in reach.

#### From a reviewed Claude game ####
* **The admin stays signed in across updates.** On a host without a disk every update signed everyone out, so a game against Claude silently fell back to the search bot ("not allowed"). The admin's sign-in now survives a restart. If you are signed out anyway, the note under the board says so instead of "unavailable".
* **A weak Avatar stays home.** The search bot walked its Lavamancer (power 1) onto enemy sites to hit them for 1 and died there. A weak Avatar standing off its own ground is now counted against the position, except when the opponent is at Death's Door.
* **No more empty activations.** The bot no longer uses an ability that does nothing (Toolbox with no spell it can pay for).
* **Fix: "target location" spells on an occupied square.** When a spell cast from a Toolbox (Lightning Bolt) asked for a location, clicking a minion standing there did nothing, so a square with a minion on it could not be picked. A click on the minion now picks its square.

#### Every game is kept and reviewed ####
* **Automatic game archive.** Every finished game against the computer is now saved without pressing anything: on your own machine under `match-replays/` as before, and on the hosted site to a `game-archive` branch of the repository (needs `GAME_ARCHIVE_TOKEN` on the server). A game flagged with "flag for review" goes to the review queue there too.
* **Engine report.** When a game ends, the rules engine replays it and records the tempo of every turn (sites, life, hand, minions on each side), the stretches where the position swung the most, and what the search engine would play today at the computer's worst moments. It is saved with the game.
* **Written review.** Where the Claude opponent is available, Claude writes a short review of the finished game from that report and the log: the key moments, one thing for you, and the computer's weaknesses. It appears at the bottom left when the game ends and is saved with the game. This works at every computer level and uses a few thousand tokens of your API credit per game.
* A Claude game's saved file also holds everything Claude was shown and chose, so the "Save game for review" download is no longer needed for that.

#### Sealed practice against the computer ####
* **Sealed practice.** Under "Play vs Computer": pick a set and a number of boosters (6 by default), open them one at a time, build a deck of at least 24 spells and 12 sites with the supplied Spellslinger and basic sites, and play. No timer and no second player needed. It uses the difficulty chosen above, so you can practise against Fast, Thinking or Claude.
* **The computer builds a sealed deck too.** It opens its own boosters and builds the way a careful player would: the one or two elements whose best 24 spells are strongest, mostly minions with plenty of cheap plays, the best few removal spells, at most four expensive cards, and a 15-site atlas split by how much of each element its spells need. Card ratings are nudged by the Play Sorcery Online win-rate statistics.
* **💡 Suggest a build.** Once all boosters are open, this fills your deck with what the computer's builder would make from your pool, with a short note on elements, sites and curve. Change it as you like.
* **↺ Same boosters again** rebuilds from your last sealed pool against the same computer deck, to try a different build.
* **Larger card preview while building a sealed deck.** The card under the mouse is now shown large in the middle of the screen instead of in a narrow column at the right edge.
* **Deck dashboard while building.** The sealed deck screen now has a strip of small charts above the card pool, updating as you add and remove cards: **totals** (spells, sites, average cost against the 24 and 12 minimums), the **mana curve** (minions and other spells in different colours), **minions and other spells** (the split, plus a bar each for Minions, Magic, Auras and Artifacts), and **thresholds** (for each element, how many spells need it against how much your sites provide, with the highest threshold any spell needs; an element your sites cannot reach, or barely reach, is marked). The regular deck builder's analysis gained the card-type and threshold charts too.
* **Fix: no tokens in boosters.** Sealed boosters (practice and online) could contain token cards: Skeleton, Frog, Foot Soldier, Bruin, Tawny and Rubble. Tokens are only ever made by other cards and are no longer in packs.
* **Sort the card pool.** The sealed deck screen has a sort menu next to the filters: by name, by mana cost, by element, or by element and then cost. Your deck list follows the same order and shows each spell's cost, and the choice is remembered.
* **Sealed practice keeps your deck.** The deck you build is saved as you go. When a sealed practice game ends, the button on the game-over panel is now **✎ Edit deck and play again**: it takes you back to your pool with the deck as you left it, to change it and play the same computer deck again. After a reload or a visit to the home page, **↺ Continue my sealed deck** brings back the pool and the deck (it used to start from an empty deck).
* **Resizable sealed screen.** The booster area now grows to show every card of the booster at once (it used to scroll inside a short box). Three drag handles let you size the screen: under the booster area (its height), at the left edge of your deck list (its width), and under the card pool (the height of the pool and deck list). Double-click a handle to go back to the default. A **📊 Hide charts** button folds the deck charts away. Your sizes are remembered.
* **Deck import reads pasted lists.** The deck builder's text import now also understands a list copied from a chat or a web page: table rows (`| 4 | Bosk Troll | Alpha/Beta | Ordinary |`), bold headings (Minions, Magic, Atlas, Collection), an `Avatar: Name` line, and a set or rarity written after a card's name. If several decks are pasted at once, the first is imported.
* **Fix: Hailstriker could not be cast with a target.** Its Genesis targets an adjacent location, and the board had no way to pick a location for a Genesis: a click on a square did nothing and a click on a minion was refused as an invalid target. After choosing where to summon it, click a square next to it (or a minion or site on that square).
* **The game archive says when it fails.** If the archive is on but a finished game cannot be saved, a note at the top left now says so and why (for example, a GitHub token without write access). The status check at `/api/local-replay` also reports the last save: when, which file, or the error.

#### From two more reviewed Claude games ####
* **Fix: Claude lost turns to a crash.** When the search engine's plan for the turn cast a minion and then moved it, describing that plan to Claude failed ("Cannot read properties of undefined") and the search bot played instead. The plan is now described step by step as it would unfold.
* **Claude is told about ranged threats.** The position now names any enemy Ranged unit with a clear line of fire to its Avatar (including Sissi Marksmen shooting across the edge of the realm) and says what to do about it. In one game a single Marksmen dealt 12 of the Deathspeaker's 20 life over six turns without ever being answered.
* **Claude's guidance** now says not to pass with good attacks available just to "hold position", to deal with recurring damage first, and to check what the opponent gains from an effect that applies to both players (it cast Courtesan Thaïs and you used its turn to destroy its own sites).
* **Deathspeaker** only offers dead minions it can pay for; choosing one it could not afford did nothing.
* **Legion of Gall** can only carry off cards that are really in the collection it is looking at. Before, a computer opponent could name a card that was not there and waste a pick, and its picks could come from a plan made for the other collection.
* **Hailstriker** lights up the squares you can pick for its Genesis.
* **Computer opponent no longer hangs after a site update.** If the planner cannot start (the site was updated while your tab was open) or stops answering, the game carries on in a simpler mode and a note asks you to reload.
* **Game review** now says why when Claude's write-up fails instead of disappearing, keeps the reason with the saved game, and allows the write-up more time.
* **Lacuna Entity** (and other Genesis effects that target a minion): clicking the site or square under the target now picks it when it is the only legal target there; before, only a click on the small unit chip worked.
* **Game review** gets more room to write, and an empty answer now reports what came back.
